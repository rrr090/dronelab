How it works

The project grew in three stages, and each one has its own test suite and a hand-written controller to beat.

1. Hover

One planar quadrotor holds its position under pushes and motor faults.

2. Cargo

Three 3D quadrotors share a load on elastic ropes. In a storm stronger than anything in training, the trained team succeeded in 75% of 8 test episodes and the baseline in 12%.

3. Mission

The team flies the cargo through a generated map to a goal pad. The score is the cargo's health on delivery.

The learned correction

Learning to fly from nothing scored below the baseline, so the network now starts from it. With a zero correction the team flies exactly like the baseline, and training only has to find improvements.

The first version of this also lost. With a correction three times larger, three training seeds scored 49, 78 and 69 against the baseline's 74, and the cargo was shaken apart by rope jerk. Shrinking the correction and keeping the best checkpoint on validation maps brought every seed to 75.5–76.8 on those same test maps. Because that choice was made while looking at those maps, later results come from new ones.

Rope jerk was still the main source of damage, so the network was given peak rope tension and past frames as inputs, plus a penalty that starts before damage does. All three seeds beat the baseline, by 6.2 points on average (95% interval +4.3 to +8.5). Both changes went in together, so this does not show how much each one contributed. Three further runs then continued from the best seed at full difficulty, and six more continued from the best of those. The best of the six is the policy shown here. On the maps above it gains 2.8 points (95% interval +1.7 to +4.4) over the model it replaced, mostly through less rope jerk.

Physics

Rigid-body quadrotors and a cargo on spring-damper ropes that can go slack and snap tight, written in vectorized NumPy.

Maps

Forest, city, gate, storm and mixed maps are generated from a seed. An A* planner finds a route wide enough for the formation.

Training

PPO written from scratch, with 64 parallel environments and a curriculum that ramps up wind, gusts and map density.

Cargo health

Starts at 100 and drops with rope jerks, high g-forces, impacts and ropes rubbing on obstacles.

What the network learned

The analysis looks for neurons that track cargo swing, rope tension and obstacles, comparing each against untrained networks of the same shape. No single neuron beat them for swing or tension, and only a few did for obstacles.

Holding the top 32 tension-tuned neurons at their average while flying cost 2.3 points (95% interval −3.6 to −1.2) and raised rope-jerk damage from 5.8 to 8.0, against 0.7 points for each of two sets of neurons with similar influence. The previous chosen model showed the same pattern, but this one was trained from it, so that is not an independent replication. Swing neurons cost 1.9 points against no loss and 0.8 for their controls, which is suggestive but was not clear in the previous model. Obstacle neurons cost no more than their controls. This is one model on 60 maps with two control sets per concept, so it is evidence, not proof.

Limitations

This is a learning project, not a validated flight model. The drones observe their state perfectly, motors respond instantly, the cargo is a point mass, and there is no aerodynamic interaction between drones. Results describe this simulator only.
